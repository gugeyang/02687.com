## How I evaluated these / What matters for safety training software

When I'm evaluating online safety training platforms for a client, I'm not just looking at a feature checklist. I'm focused on the outcomes that prevent incidents and pass audits. A general-purpose corporate LMS often fails here because it lacks the specific DNA needed for safety and compliance.

Here are the four pillars of my evaluation process:

1.  **Audit-Proof Reporting and Certification Management:** This is non-negotiable. Can I, with three clicks, pull a report showing every forklift operator who has a certification expiring in the next 30 days? Can the system automatically re-enroll them? I look for granular reporting, clear audit trails, and automated lifecycle management for certifications. If this is your main concern, you should also read my guide on the [best compliance training LMS for regulated industries](/blog/best-compliance-training-lms-for-regulated-industries/), which goes deeper into this topic.

2.  **Field-Ready Mobile Experience:** Safety doesn't happen at a desk. Training, incident reports, and checklist verifications happen on the factory floor, at a construction site, or in a vehicle. I test the mobile app rigorously. Can a supervisor easily conduct a toolbox talk and mark attendance on their phone? Can a technician access a quick "lockout/tagout" refresher video right at the machine? A clunky or offline-incapable mobile app is a deal-breaker for many of my clients.

3.  **Integrated EHS Workflow:** The best safety training software doesn't live in a silo. It's part of a larger Environmental, Health, and Safety (EHS) ecosystem. I prioritize platforms that integrate training data with incident management, safety observations, and corrective action plans. For example, if a near-miss is reported, can you automatically assign a new micro-learning module to the entire team involved? That's the difference between a passive learning tool and an active safety system.

4.  **Relevant, High-Quality Content:** Does the platform come with a library of up-to-date, OSHA-compliant courses? How easy is it to create my own site-specific training? While some organizations have their own content, I've found that a solid, pre-built library from providers like HSI or SafetySkills can save hundreds of hours and ensure content is vetted by actual safety professionals.

## Pricing reality

Virtually every serious player in the safety software space uses a **quote-based pricing model**. You won't find a public pricing page. Why? Because the final price depends on multiple factors:

*   **Number of active users:** This is the primary driver.
*   **Included modules:** Are you just buying the LMS, or the full EHS suite with incident management, audits, etc.?
*   **Content library access:** Getting their full safety course library is often a separate, significant line item.
*   **Implementation and support:** Expect a one-time setup fee and potentially tiered support packages.

Don't be shy during the sales process. Ask for a detailed breakdown of all costs. A per-user license structure, like that used by some content providers, can seem straightforward but get expensive fast if you have a large number of infrequent users. Plan for a total cost of ownership that includes software, content, and internal administration time.

## FAQ

**Do I need a dedicated safety platform or can a general LMS work?**
A general LMS like Absorb or Docebo can work well, especially if you have a strong internal EHS team to manage content and compliance. However, dedicated platforms like HSI or VelocityEHS offer integrated incident reporting and audit tools that a general LMS won't have.

**What's the difference between an LMS and an EHS platform?**
An LMS (Learning Management System) is focused on delivering and tracking training. An EHS (Environment, Health, and Safety) platform is a broader suite that manages incident reports, risk assessments, safety audits, and chemical inventories, with training being just one module.

**How important is a pre-built course library?**
Extremely. Unless you have a dedicated instructional design team, creating high-quality, regulation-compliant safety courses is a massive undertaking. A good library from a vendor like HSI or SafetySkills can accelerate your program by months and reduce legal risk.

**Can these platforms track hands-on training and certifications?**
Yes, all the platforms listed can. You can create "in-person" or "observational checklist" activities in the system, which a certified instructor or manager can then sign off on, creating a digital record in the user's transcript alongside their online coursework.
