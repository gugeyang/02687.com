## How I evaluated these / What matters for employee training software

When I evaluate a learning management system for employee training, I don't just look at the feature list. I focus on the factors that determine whether the platform will be adopted and deliver real business value, or just become expensive shelfware.

Here are my core criteria:

*   **Learner Experience (LX):** Is it intuitive and engaging for employees? If the interface is clunky or looks like it was designed in 2005, your adoption rates will suffer. People expect a Netflix-like experience, and while no LMS is quite there, some get much closer than others.
*   **Admin Experience (AX):** How much time will your team spend managing it? I look for efficient user management, intuitive reporting dashboards, and straightforward course creation workflows. A bad admin panel can burn hundreds of hours a year.
*   **Integrations:** No LMS is an island. It must connect to your core business systems: your HRIS (for user provisioning), Salesforce (for sales training), Slack/Teams (for notifications and in-workflow learning), and more. I prioritize platforms with robust, well-documented APIs and pre-built connectors.
*   **Reporting and Analytics:** Can you actually measure the impact of your training? I need to see more than just completion rates. I'm looking for learner progress tracking, assessment scores, engagement metrics, and the ability to link training outcomes to performance data.
*   **Content Authoring:** Does the platform have a decent built-in authoring tool, or does it force you to rely on external tools like Articulate or iSpring? For rapid content creation by subject-matter experts (SMEs), a good internal tool is non-negotiable.
*   **Scalability & Total Cost of Ownership:** Will this platform grow with you? And what's the *real* price? I look past the sticker price to consider implementation fees, support packages, and per-user costs that can explode as you scale.

## Pricing Reality: How to Decode the Sales Pitch

LMS pricing can be intentionally confusing. There are three main models you'll encounter:

1.  **Per Active User/Month:** (e.g., 360Learning). This seems straightforward, but be clear on the definition of "active." Is it anyone who logs in? Anyone enrolled in a course? This model is predictable but can get expensive if you have a large number of infrequent learners.
2.  **Tiered Plans:** (e.g., TalentLMS). This is common for SMB-focused platforms. You pay a flat fee for a certain number of users and features (e.g., $119/month for up to 40 users). This is great for starting small, but watch out for key features being locked behind expensive higher tiers.
3.  **Quote-Based Annual Contracts:** (e.g., Docebo, Absorb, LearnUpon, Cornerstone). This is the enterprise standard. The price is based on user numbers, feature modules, implementation services, and support levels. **Everything is negotiable.** Never accept the first quote. The real price is often significantly lower, but you have to go through a lengthy sales cycle to find it.

## FAQ: Questions I Hear from Every Client

*   **How long does implementation *really* take?**
    For something like TalentLMS, a small company can be live in a week. For an enterprise platform like Cornerstone or Docebo, plan for a 3-9 month project involving IT, HR, and content teams. The technical setup is only one part; content migration and change management take time.

*   **Can I create courses directly in these platforms?**
    Yes, all of them have built-in authoring tools for creating simple courses with text, images, video, and quizzes. For highly interactive, simulation-based training, many companies still use dedicated tools like Articulate Storyline and import the content as a SCORM package.

*   **Do I need a dedicated person to manage the LMS?**
    For platforms like TalentLMS, a tech-savvy HR generalist can manage it part-time. For Docebo, Absorb, or Cornerstone, you absolutely need at least one full-time dedicated LMS administrator. Underestimating the admin workload is one of the biggest mistakes I see clients make.
